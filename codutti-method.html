The Codutti Method

A walk through the Pasian di Prato and Martignacco workshops — where wood, metal and leather are cut, shaped and finished entirely in-house, and where seventy years of craftsmanship now run alongside CNC precision.

Two Workshops, One Standard

Every Codutti piece is born and finished across two sites in Friuli Venezia Giulia: the Pasian di Prato headquarters, where design, sales and administration come together, and the Martignacco production plant, where wood, metal and leather are actually turned into furniture. Nothing is outsourced — from the first cut to the final stitch, the same team that designs a piece is the one that builds it, under one roof, to one standard.

The Machinery

Panel saws and edge-banders cut and finish every wood component to size, while multi-axis CNC routers carve profiles, joints and cable routings with a repeatability hand tools can't match. Powder-coating and painting lines finish metal frames and structures, and an in-house upholstery department — cutting tables, industrial sewing machines, and hand-stitching benches — turns hides into the leather finishes seen across the Executive range. Every machine on the floor exists to remove variance from the parts a craftsperson can't control, so their hands stay free for the parts that matter.

Where the Hand Still Leads

Technology sets the tolerances; it doesn't make the decisions. Veneers are matched and bookmatched by eye before they're cut, leather hides are inspected and laid out to avoid natural marks, and every hand-stitched seam — the detail most associated with Codutti's Executive collections — is still sewn by an upholsterer trained inside the company, not by a machine. That balance, machine precision paired with a craftsperson's judgement, is what the Codutti Method actually is.
